# Academic evidence summaries

Prepared 2026-09-29 from the saved source ledger, primary papers, author-deposited bibliographic abstracts, and targeted primary-source checks. Each paragraph distinguishes demonstrated results from inference. DOI links identify publications; local paths identify the evidence actually available in this workspace. The institutional directory is an evidence map rather than a statement that all listed groups currently operate molten-glass printers.

**Klein et al. (2015), `acad_klein2015`.** [Additive Manufacturing of Optically Transparent Glass](https://doi.org/10.1089/3dp.2015.0021), *3D Printing and Additive Manufacturing* 2, 92–105, establishes reservoir-fed extrusion of transparent glass and the original MIT G3DP architecture. The saved author manuscript reports geometry, interlayer adhesion, optical transmission and deliberately shaped caustics. It supports feasibility and the connection between thermal control, viscosity and deposition; visible transparency is not equivalent to imaging-optic surface quality or a structural design allowables database. Evidence: `sources/papers/2015_Klein_Additive_Manufacturing_of_Optically_Transparent_Glass.pdf` and matching extracted text. Nozzle chemistry differs in wording between the institutional page and manuscript, so descriptions should remain attributed to their specific source.

**Inamura et al. (2018), `acad_inamura2018`.** [Additive Manufacturing of Transparent Glass Structures](https://doi.org/10.1089/3dp.2018.0157), volume 5, 269–283, describes G3DP2 with independently coordinated heating zones and four-axis motion. Its architectural demonstration comprises assembled columns, rather than evidence that an individual printer built a monolithic structure of the displayed total height. This is a central source for integrated furnace, delivery and motion design and reported mechanical characterization; it does not establish universal structural qualification. Evidence: saved paper and matching text under `sources/papers/2018_Inamura_*` and `sources/data/text/2018_Inamura_*`.

**von Witzendorff et al. (2018), `acad_witzendorff2018`.** [CO2-Laser glass deposition printing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procir.2018.08.109), *Procedia CIRP* 74, 272–275, demonstrates direct quartz-fiber deposition using localized laser heating and a translated substrate. It is evidence for a direct high-temperature route without a large molten-glass reservoir and for the linked roles of feedstock delivery, optical heating and motion. Statements envisioning replacement of manual manufacture are application proposals, not proof of industrial production yield or medical qualification. Evidence: saved four-page conference paper and extracted text.

**Liu, Oriekhov and Fokine (2022), `acad_liu2022`.** [Investigation of glass bonding and multi-layer deposition during filament-based glass 3D printing](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmats.2022.978861), *Frontiers in Materials* 9, 978861, systematically varies speed, feed rate and laser power for approximately 196 µm fused-silica filament. The study measures contact width separately from deposited track width and demonstrates multilayer deposition; its reported bond widths depend on operating conditions. These measurements substantiate geometric bonding control, not universal tensile strength or a commercial resolution rating. Evidence: saved paper/text and publisher PDF; author affiliations are KTH and Nobula, while the editor's RISE affiliation is not an apparatus attribution.

**Baudet et al. (2019), `acad_baudet2019`.** [3D-printing of arsenic sulfide chalcogenide glasses](https://doi.org/10.1364/OME.9.002307), *Optical Materials Express* 9(5), 2307–2317, demonstrates direct filament-fed extrusion of an arsenic-sulfide glass on a modified commercial printer at Laval. Its lower processing window and infrared-material context must remain distinct from silicate and fused-silica extrusion. The paper is relevant to composition-specific printability and specialty optics, not evidence that the same apparatus processes all glass families. Evidence available here: Crossref bibliographic record and the [publisher's article description](https://opg.optica.org/spotlight/summary.cfm?id=409127); no local full-paper PDF was identified in the resumed inventory, so additional quantitative extraction should first acquire the paper.

**Nguyen et al. (2017), `acad_nguyen2017`.** [3D-Printed Transparent Glass](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.201701181), *Advanced Materials* 29, 1701181, reports silica-particle inks deposited by direct ink writing, then dried and densified thermally. It establishes transparent shapes made by a multistage route at temperatures below silica melting, and therefore provides an important indirect-process comparator. The word “direct” in DIW does not mean direct molten-glass deposition. Evidence: publisher-deposited abstract and bibliographic metadata in `sources/data/acad_nguyen2017_crossref.json`; no full local paper was identified, so this paragraph makes no new numerical optical or strength claim.

**Destino et al. (2018), `acad_destino2018`.** [3D Printed Optical Quality Silica and Silica–Titania Glasses from Sol–Gel Feedstocks](https://doi.org/10.1002/admt.201700323), *Advanced Materials Technologies* 3, 1700323, combines sol-derived inks, extrusion, drying and controlled thermal processing. The reported comparison with commercial optical glasses includes composition, optical properties and polished surface quality. It supports the prospect of optical-grade AM after the specified processing chain; it must not be cited as optical performance of an untreated molten cord. Evidence: saved publisher abstract in Crossref metadata and [author institution's publication record](https://researchworks.creighton.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/3D-Printed-Optical-Quality-Silica-and/991005930846702656). Local full text was not identified.

**Chen et al. (2025 issue; online 2024), `acad_chen2025`.** [Temperature Control of Quartz-Glass Melting Areas in Laser Additive Manufacturing](https://doi.org/10.3390/mi16010029), *Micromachines* 16, 29, reports a PID temperature-control loop that changes laser power during quartz-glass deposition. Comparison with constant-power experiments supports improved formation under the studied setup. The work motivates closed-loop heating but does not validate transfer of controller settings, emissivity assumptions or power limits to another machine. Evidence: saved Crossref abstract plus publisher and [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39858685/); online publication is 28 December 2024. No newly downloaded full PDF was required for the limited claims used here.

**Chhadeh et al. (2026), `acad_chhadeh2026`.** [The effect of toolpath design on the mechanical strength of additively manufactured glass components](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44334-026-00072-1), *npj Advanced Manufacturing* 3, 16, is a verified 3 April 2026 paper. It uses pulled soda-lime rods and a Maple 4 printer, then compares specimen orientations using four-point bending. The saved Methods give 940 °C at the nozzle-tip thermocouple, a 500 °C chamber and 1.5 mm nozzle outlet. The approximately 40% greater mean strength for 90° than 0° specimens is a within-study result coupled to toolpath-dependent thermal history; it is not a universal anisotropy factor. The authors explicitly describe deviations from EN 1288-3. Evidence: saved publisher HTML `sources/data/asset_http/chhadeh2026.html`, saved PDF, DOI metadata and live Crossmark confirmation.

**Stern et al. (2024), `acad_stern2024`.** [Advancing Sustainable 3D Printing: The Feasibility of Recycled Glass as a Building Material With Additive Manufacturing](https://doi.org/10.47982/cgc.9.583), *Challenging Glass Conference Proceedings* 9, compares studio, recycled container and recycled float glass through viscosity and extrusion behavior and uses infrared thermography. It supports feedstock-specific process characterization, not the proposition that arbitrary mixed cullet is interchangeable. Authors are affiliated with Evenline and MIT; the conference's TU Delft venue does not establish Delft ownership of the printer. Evidence: publisher abstract in saved Crossref metadata and [primary conference paper](https://proceedings.challengingglass.com/index.php/cgc/article/download/583/483/2087).

**Liu et al. (2024), `com_liu`.** [Rapid Fabrication of Silica Microlens Arrays via Glass 3D Printing](https://doi.org/10.1089/3dp.2022.0112), *3D Printing and Additive Manufacturing* 11(2), 460–466, is relevant to direct laser filament deposition of micro-optical elements. Its microlens-array demonstration and characterization support application-specific optical fabrication rather than a claim that every printed shape is an imaging optic. Chapter 07 attributes laser postprocessing and manufacturer affiliations, both necessary qualifications when discussing process maturity. Evidence: saved commercial source record and live [KTH institutional publication listing](https://kth.diva-portal.org/smash/person.jsf?pid=authority-person:74530); full-paper numerical surface-quality comparisons should be checked against actual metrology conditions before entering the report.

**Massimino et al. (2024), `com_masonry`.** [Additive manufacturing of interlocking glass masonry units](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40940-024-00279-8), *Glass Structures & Engineering* 9, 397–417, studies Evenline G3DP3-derived masonry units and interlocking assembly. It substantiates an architectural research direction with specimen-level evidence, not a general building-product certification or qualified arbitrary structure. Different fabrication treatments of contact/interlock regions matter to interpreting mechanical performance. Evidence: local primary paper, text and saved publisher HTML; company-affiliated contributors are disclosed rather than described as independent customer acceptance testing.

**SCHOTT TIE-27 (2023), `acad_schott2023`.** [Stress in optical glass](https://media.schott.com/api/public/content/d33171e170af4b94a8e49b11d758258e?v=610aa3b1) supplies manufacturer technical guidance on annealing, stress birefringence and geometric dependence. It anchors interpretation of residual stress and conventional relaxation ranges, with viscosity conversion explicitly preserved: 10^13 dPa·s is 10^12 Pa·s. It is not an experimentally qualified cooling schedule for any printed geometry. Evidence in this review is the saved academic source record and chapter extraction; this resumed review did not independently redownload the technical note.

**Heraeus glass-manufacturing equipment, `acad_heraeus`.** The [supplier's component guidance](https://www.heraeus-precious-metals.com/en/products-solutions/category/precious-metal-components/glass-manufacturing-equipment/) provides context for platinum and alloy selection according to glass chemistry and purity requirements. The chapter appropriately avoids a universal recommendation for Pt–Rh because optical coloration and other compatibility requirements differ. This is component-engineering guidance, not proof of nozzle lifetime in an AM system. Evidence: saved source ledger and attributed chapter notes; undated page, accessed in the original 2026-09-26 research run.
