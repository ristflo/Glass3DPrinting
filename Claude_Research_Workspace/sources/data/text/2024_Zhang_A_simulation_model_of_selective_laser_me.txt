A simulation model of selective laser melting (SLM) of glass silica 
monolayer
Wanrui Zhang1 
Received: 20 September 2023 / Accepted: 21 January 2024

Abstract
Although experimental research demonstrates the feasibility of processing glass via selective laser melting (SLM), achiev -
ing SLM precision manufacturing of fused silica is still a challenging but promising field. Therefore, a numerical model 
was developed in this work to predict the densification kinetics of glass powder monolayer under SLM, which provided a 
simulation tool for the optimization design of processing parameters. To be particular, the relative neck diameter and density 
of the powder bed under SLM were calculated using a cluster model to simulate the degree of consolidation, from which the 
thermal diffusivity of the powder bed was determined and the corresponding results of heat transfer were obtained through 
the numerical model implemented by using finite difference method (FDM). The experimental results for the SLM of glass 
with different processing parameters from other references validated the simulation results with a prediction error of less 
than 2%. Hence, the effects of processing settings, such as the influence of particle size and preheating temperature, could 
be accurately investigated by using this numerical modelling method. It was found that using powders with smaller particle 
sizes and setting a higher preheat temperature could improve the manufacturing quality of the obtained glass parts. From the 
densification results of the numerical modelling, we also introduced a novel optimization criterion, which could be utilized 
to optimise the processing parameters and fabricate the high-purity glass parts via SLM.
Keywords Selective laser melting (SLM) · Silica glass · Simulation model · Processing optimisation
1 Introduction
Additive manufacturing (AM), or 3D printing, is a manu-
facturing technology in which a three-dimensional object is 
constructed by adding material layer by layer [ 1]. The AM 
technique in this study is selective laser melting (SLM) which 
melts the powder bed from bottom to upper and produces 
a fully dense part layer-wise [2 ]. Accurate and rapid fabri-
cation is the most outstanding strength of SLM over other 
AM methods, allowing the precision processing of micro-
and nano-powders [3]. However, even though glass–ceramic 
material has many desirable properties, such as low thermal 
expansion coefficient, low electrical conductivity and high 
chemical resistance, applying the technique of SLM on glass 
is still challenging [4]. The main impediment hindering the 
development of SLM for glass is the infamous difficulty in 
shaping glass parts, which demands high temperatures and a 
complex casting process [5].
Over the past few years, several experimental investiga-
tions have attempted to fabricate different glass ceramic 
parts using SLM. Fateri and Gebhardt [6 ] demonstrate 
SLM’s feasibility for soda-lime glass powder and fabricated 
parts with a relative density of 93%. An experimental study 
on quartz glass conducted by Khmyrov et al. [7] successfully 
fabricates a crack-free glass monolayer using SLM. Moreo-
ver, Gan and Wong [8] successfully use SLM to build porous 
lithium aluminosilicate (LAS) glass parts with spodumene 
minerals. Although not mature enough to manufacture the 
ideal glass ceramic objects, these studies are essential for 
the development of SLM for glass materials in the future.
In addition to the experimental studies of SLM for glass, 
simulation is an essential method that provides a deeper under-
standing of this laser AM process of glass ceramics. However, 
only a handful of numerical investigations are available for 
the SLM of glass or ceramic materials. To predict the thermal 
distribution and melt-pool geometry, Li et al. [9] put forward

the consolidation process of silica powder bed by considering 
the effective thermal conductivity related to the neck growth 
between glass particles.
However, little modelling study to date focuses on the 
densification process of the glass powder bed, causing the 
lack of criteria to optimise the processing parameters and 
limiting the fabrication resolution of SLM. Specifically, 
the thermal diffusivity of the silica powder layer is highly 
affected by its densification degree, determining the effec -
tiveness of SLM processing on glass [12]. The simulation 
for the densification of silica powder is also indispensable 
since the properties of the finally obtained glass part highly 
depend on its relative density [13]. Besides, research on 
the densification process of powder monolayer under SLM 
solidifies the foundation of quantitively calculating the 
shrinkage deformation of the glass compact. Thus, a numeri-
cal model of SLM for silica glass was established in this 
work to predict the densification and deformation kinetics 
of the glass compact.
In this work, the sintering densification kinetics of the 
glass powders are incorporated into our numerical model 
using the Cluster model [14] during SLM. Before reaching 
the melting point, sintering occurred between glass powders 
due to the high temperature. Accordingly, the area between 
the fully dense glass compact and the initially loose powder 
bed is a zone of density variation dominated by glass sinter-
ing. Additionally, plenty of research demonstrates the Cluster 
model’s feasibility on various glass materials [15, 16]. Hence, 
this study established a heat conduction model with sinter -
ing effects for SLM of silica glass to predict its densifica-
tion process, which is numerically implemented by using the 
finite difference method (FDM). Similar to the previous inves-
tigations done by other researchers [7 , 9–12], we focus our 
numerical study on the glass monolayer in the SLM process, 
which is fundamental for the research of parts fabrication.

As depicted in Fig. 9, the thermal gradient is reduced at the 
initial ambient temperature. Figure 9 also illustrates that the 
densification process is accelerated for the glass monolayer 
under SLM. Thus, it is concluded that preheating can enhance 
the quality of the final glass part due to the lower thermal 
gradient and increase the manufacturing efficiency of SLM.
3.4  Processing optimisation
The densification results shown in this numerical study 
agreed well with the experimentally observed micrographs 
in Fig. 6. The dark yellow band in Fig.  6, representing the 
relative density from 1 to 0.95, could be matched with the 
boundary line between the fused glass and the non-consol-
idated glass. At the bottom of the monolayer in Fig.  6, the 
point between the consolidated glass and non-consolidated 
powders was defined as the critical point of the bottom. 
Meanwhile, this kind of point at the top surface was defined 
as the critical point of the top. As illustrated in Fig.  10, the 
distance between the two critical points was defined as the 
critical distance, which could work as an optimisation cri-
terion. In other words, the closer the distance is, the better 
the SLM quality is.
Thus, a new optimisation scheme could be made 
according to this observation. The design variables were 
the parameters such as laser power, scanning velocity, 
layer thickness, particle size and ambient temperature. 
Minimising the critical distance was identified as the 
objective function. Additionally, the constraints included 
the maximum or minimum values of the variables, such 
as the range of the laser power, the required width scope 
of the fused glass and the maximum ambient temperature 
for preheating. We could take Fig.  10 as an example of the 
optimisation scheme established in this study. The densi-
fication results of case (a) were optimised by increasing 
ambient temperature from 298 to 498 K and decreasing 
particle size from 20 to 10 µm.
4  Conclusion
In this study, a numerical model was established to predict the 
temperature distribution, densification kinetics and deformation 
of the monolayer of glass powders under SLM. Specifically, the 
necking growth and densification kinetics were incorporated 
into the heat transfer model, which was calculated using FDM. 
The experimental observations from other research could vali-
date the prediction results of the newly developed model with a 
prediction error of less than 2%. Thus, the well-proved numeri-
cal simulation could provide a more reliable tool to discuss the 
influence of the processing parameters. It was noted that the 
smaller size of the powders and the higher ambient temperature 
can speed up the consolidation process and improve the qual-
ity of the obtained parts. Besides, a new optimisation criterion 
based on the model can be set to optimise the parameters and 
fabricate the transparent and high-purity glass through SLM. In 
the future, researchers can use this numerical modelling method 
for glass monolayer to aid their 3D design and fabrication of 
fused silica parts by using SLM.
